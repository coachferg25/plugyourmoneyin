Is it safe to plug your money into an AI?

Short answer: looking is much safer than acting. Here is how to keep it that way.

The three levels of access

  1. Read-only. The AI can see balances and transactions. It cannot move money. ChatGPT Finances and Grok's Finance integration work this way, through Plaid.
  2. Read + edit. Budgeting apps like Monarch can offer an option that lets the AI change data inside the app. Keep it off unless you need it.
  3. Can trade. Brokers like Robinhood, Public and Coinbase let an AI place real orders. A mistake can cost real money.

Rules of thumb

  • Never type your bank or brokerage password into a chat or give it to an AI agent. Banks such as Chase say they block agents that log in as you.
  • Connect through the company's own sign-in screen (OAuth or Plaid), so the AI never sees your password.
  • Start read-only. Add trading only in a small separate account with money you can afford to lose.
  • Prefer a company's official connector over a third-party one. Third-party servers may hold your tokens or data.
  • Check which accounts you shared and revoke what you don't use (your bank's “linked apps” page, and Plaid's portal).
  • An AI can be wrong. Double-check numbers before acting, and be careful with anything that can place an order.
  • Read the privacy settings. For example, OpenAI says it does not train on your Finances data; confirm current terms yourself.

Why “Partial” is common

Banks and brokers decide what is allowed. Some block logins by AI agents but allow customer-approved data sharing. Rules are changing fast, and a US federal data-sharing rule (CFPB section 1033) is currently on hold during a rewrite. See what changed.
